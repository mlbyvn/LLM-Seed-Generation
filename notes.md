## Abstract

The performance of a fuzzer on the same program can vary significantly depending on the quality and nature of the seed inputs. Even valid but different seeds can lead to vastly different behaviors. Recent researches have shown that seeds generated by large language models (LLMs) can enhance mutation-based fuzzing by increasing the discovery of crashes and vulnerabilities in software.
Fuzzing smart contracts, however, poses unique challenges compared to traditional software, primarily due to their state-dependent behavior and the economic and environmental considerations of blockchain systems.
Despite these challenges, LLM-generated seeds hold great potential for improving smart contract fuzzing, particularly by covering specific edge cases.
This thesis employs a two-fold approach to evaluate the effectiveness of LLM-generated seeds in smart contract fuzzing. The first part focuses on common security properties defined for various ERC standards, including ERC20, ERC721, and ERC4626. To analyze these properties, ERC20- and ERC721-compliant tokens with deliberate violations are implemented. These contracts are fuzzed using both random and LLM-generated seeds for a comparative analysis.
The second part involves testing the performance of LLM-generated seeds by fuzzing protocol-specific invariants. The aim is to assess whether LLM-generated seeds improve the discovery of invariant violations compared to traditional random seeding.
This dual approach provides a comprehensive evaluation of LLM-generated seeds in uncovering vulnerabilities in both standard-specific properties and broader protocol invariants.

## Dataset of contracts

One common issue with existing datasets is that they feature contracts with outdated Solidity versions (ranging from 0.4.24 to 0.7). Despite Solidity 0.8.0 being introduced in late 2020, there remains a lack of large datasets containing vulnerable contracts utilizing pragma versions above 0.8.0. To address this problem, several approaches were considered:

1. **Refactor existing datasets**  
   - **Approach:** Use contracts from current datasets and rewrite them to support newer pragma versions.  
   - **Drawbacks:** This is highly labor-intensive and introduces a risk of unintentionally adding new bugs during the refactoring process.  

2. **Generate contracts with bug synthesizers**  
   - **Approach:** Use automated tools to create vulnerable contracts.  
   - **Drawbacks:** The generated contracts tend to be overly simplistic, lacking the use of libraries, ERC standards, or real-world complexity.  

3. **Leverage real-world vulnerabilities and known exploits**  
   - **Approach:** Extract real-world vulnerabilities from audit reports on platforms like [Solodit](https://solodit.cyfrin.io/) and supplement them with "textbook" bugs from Capture The Flag (CTF) challenges. These exploits can then be manually injected into sample contracts.  
   - **Advantages:** This method ensures the inclusion of realistic vulnerabilities while keeping the dataset concise and practical.
